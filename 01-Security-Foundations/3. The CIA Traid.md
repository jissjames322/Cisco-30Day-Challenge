# Confidentiality

- Protecting information from unauthorized access.( general )
- Preservation authorized restrictions on  information access and disclosure, including means for protecting personal privacy and proprietary information - NIST


Defining a data making sure there is an authorization scheme or process.

### Examples of Confidentiality Attacks


- An unauthorized user who is able to access data that they should not be able to access.

Like : Permissions not being set correctly for folders or configuration issues, 

- An adversary that uses an authorized account to access or view protected information by compromising the account and misusing the access provided.

Like : A hacker has hacked an account and viewing the data.


#### Confidentiality Controls

- Authentication  ( this includes passwords, passkeys , MFA , etc. )
- Encryption ( often tied to an authorized user identity or role )


## Integrity


- Data should be maintained in an expected state , and nobody should be able to improperly modify it, either accidentally or maliciously.

- Guarding against improper information modification or destruction and ensuring information non-repudiation and authenticity - NIST

### Examples of Integrity Attacks

- Modification of the configuration of a device or security control or by an unintended user.

- Changing data to benefit the adversary

- Changing  data to harm the victim.

####  Integrity Controls

- Logging of transactions , changes access of data

- Monitoring or alerting on unexpected access or modification of a file or data

- Periodic validation of files ( usually by matching the cryptographic hash of a file )

- Restricting installation or modification of software.



# Availability

- Authorized users should be able to access data whenever they need to do so.
- Ensuring timely and reliable access to - and use of - information.

is the data available or can the user get the data?
#### Example of Availability Attacks

- Overwhelming a process , port application or tool that has the expectation of being accessible by normal users.

- Denial Of Service or DDoS attacks

- Disasters ( Physical recovery)

#### Availability Controls

- Load Balancers and DDoS preventative infrastructure.
- Distributed and redundant systems.
- Test, maintain and monitor your key systems

# Cyber Threat Intelligence

How we learn more about our adversaries. 

## What is Intelligence

- Intelligence deals with all the things which should be known in advance initiating a course of action  (Clark Task Force ,1955 )

- Intelligence: The collection, processing , and analysis of information about a competitive entity and its agents, needed by an organization  or group for its security and well-being.

- Information and knowledge about an adversary obtained through observation, investigation , analysis , or understanding.( Joint Chiefs of Staff, 2001)


## What is CTI ( CYBER THREAT INTELLIGENCE)

- The process of collecting , analyzing and sharing information about potential and existing cyber threats and threat actors.

- To provide organizations with an understanding of cyberattacks risks, helping them to anticipate threats, improve their defenses , and respond effectively to incidents.

- Information to assist decision making that relates to threats that may damage a networked computer system.


### How do I Collect CTI ?


- Intelligence requirements cannot  be met if the data or information does not exist to properly fill the knowledge gap. ( **You need logs !**)

	The knowledge to understand logs and identify what the hacker dude was doing in our network or machine , what type of files he accessed or what payload or things he used , like executing scripts or code.

- Intrusion analysis and investigation capabilities are fundamental skills of a CTI analyst.

- Information includes attacker motivations and capabilities , tactics, techniques, procedures (TTPs), and indicators of compromise (IoCs).

	**Why? Who? How? Procedure ? IOC ?**



### Know your adversary

What to collect?


	Data +  Context = Indicator

- **Collect intrusions** :  Whether they succeeded or failed ( We mostly learn from failed attempts) we can know what security controls of our worked and failed .

# Threat Hunting

What to do with that intelligence you've gathered

### What is Threat Hunting?

- Threat Hunting is any manual or machine-assisted process for finding security incidents that your automated detection systems missed.( - Peak Threat Hunting Framework )

- The proactive and iterative process of searching organizational systems, networks, and infrastructure for advanced threats that have evaded existing security controls - NIST


### Why do Threat Hunting?


- Find previously unknown malicious activity
- Test and validate detection capabilities and identify detection gaps
- Actionable search based on new or current intelligence or events (e.g. new widely exploited vulnerability )


# The PEAK Threat Hunting Framework


- **Prepare** phase - Select topic, research , plan your hunt
- **Execute** - Implement your hunt plan
- **Act** - Documentation , automation, and communication
- **Knowledge** - Each phase learns from the prior phase as well as creating new data and findings for subsequent hunting or sharing.


### Three types of Threat Hunting

Based on the PEAK Framework


### Hypothesis-Based

"Classic" Threat Hunting based on Threat Intelligence

- Start with a hypothesis or objective
- Use data, detections, and  analysis to prove or disprove the hypothesis

### Baseline

Know normal, and then look for outliers or deviations

- Determine which data sources you will be performing the baseline against
- Scope the hunt -  a narrow hunt is  more effective
- Gather historical data for developing a statistical baseline
- Collect new/recent data for hunting against 

#### Model-Assisted

Data Science process wrapped in Threat Hunting. Heavy use of Machine Learning models.

- Scope the datasets to be used.

- Select the algorithms to use

- Gather data, Pre-Process data

- Develop model

- Refine

- Escalate critical findings


### Threat Hunting Outputs "ACT" Phase


- Hunt Documentation 
- Detection gaps and risks
- New and improved detections
- New hunt ideas
- Stakeholder reports
- Identify a Security Incident



#### Threat Hunting Conclusion?

- Powerful approach to assist with testing your detection capabilities validating the operations of your various security controls.

- Can also be used to identify undetected malicious activity within an environment.

- Can be reasonably expensive to execute. as it requires a significant amount of human analysis and involvement at all phases.
